Three drafts, ready to publish. Two tutorials and one opinion piece — the mix

| File | Type | Words |

## Read them first

**These are drafts in my voice, not yours.** Before publishing, go through each
one and change anything that does not sound like you or that you would not say
in an interview. Draft.dev will judge your writing by these, so they need to
survive the question "walk me through this piece".

Two specific things to check:

- Article 1 makes claims about your rating experience. Every claim is drawn from
  what you told me, but read it as an interviewer would and cut anything you
  could not expand on.
- Articles 2 and 3 describe the tools on your site. Make sure the code snippets
  still match what is actually deployed.

## How to publish

1. Sign up at [dev.to](https://dev.to) — GitHub login works, no approval needed
2. Click **Write a post**
3. Use the Markdown editor and paste the file contents, front matter included
   (the `---` block at the top sets title and tags automatically)
4. Change `published: false` to `published: true`
5. Publish

Post them a few days apart rather than all at once. Three articles appearing in
one hour from a brand-new account looks like content dumping.

Suggested order:

1. **Article 1** (opinion) first — it is the most distinctive and the one most
   likely to get read and shared
2. **Article 3** (prompt testing) a few days later
3. **Article 2** (the evaluator tutorial) last

## Then apply

Once all three are live, the Draft.dev Samples field takes the three dev.to
URLs. That gives them exactly what they asked for: multiple samples, a mix of
step-by-step tutorials and opinion-driven writing.

The application form is at
[draft.dev/write](https://draft.dev/write) — it is an Airtable form, not an
account signup. Other fields you will need:

- **Bio** — short, used as a byline on published articles
- **What are you learning about right now** — 1–2 paragraphs, genuine
- **Tech skills** — pick from their list; your .NET, Oracle, Python, and AI
  evaluation coverage is strong
- **Articles per month** — most of their writers do 1–2
- **Website** — your portfolio and GitHub profile

Tell me when the articles are live and I will draft the bio and the "what are
you learning" answer to match what you actually published.

## One note on expectations

Draft.dev pays well and is selective. A first application with three
self-published articles is a reasonable shot, not a certainty. If it does not
land, the same three articles work for Hashnode, LogRocket, Smashing Magazine,
and CSS-Tricks — and they make your portfolio considerably stronger regardless.
